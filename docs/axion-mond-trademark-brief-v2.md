- **Mark type:** Standard character mark (wordmark only, no stylization) — recommended for maximum protection scope. May also wish to file the stylized logo separately once finalized.

Origin: Modified Newtonian Dynamics (Milgrom 1983) — pre-existing scientific term with ~40 years of established use predating any crypto entity.
- **Mark type:** Standard character mark.
- **Sector:** Cryptocurrency / digital token / unit of account within a closed-loop economic system.
- **Deployment chain:** Base L2 (Ethereum). Base has no token-naming authority; deployment is unconstrained at the chain level. See Section 4.7.

## 3. Recommended filing strategy

### 3.1 Classes (Nice Classification)

For each mark, file in the following classes:

**Class 36 — Insurance & Financial Affairs** *(primary)*

- Financial services, monetary affairs, cryptocurrency/digital-asset issuance, electronic funds transfer, dividend distribution.
- This is the core class for both marks. Filing here is essential.

**Class 9 — Computer & Scientific Products** *(essential)*

- Downloadable software, cryptocurrency software, smart contracts, blockchain protocols, downloadable digital tokens.
- USPTO standard ID for crypto today is in Class 9.

**Class 42 — Computer & Scientific Services** *(essential)*

- SaaS, blockchain-as-a-service, software development, hosting of protocol infrastructure.

**Class 35 — Advertising & Business Services** *(recommended for AXION only)*

- Business administration of the colony economy, marketplace operation services, business consultancy. Justified by Axion’s role administering colony economies.

**Class 41 — Education & Entertainment** *(optional, AXION only)*

- The Great Collision YouTube channel and thesis publication. Defensible but not essential at first filing.

**Total at minimum:** AXION in 4 classes (9, 35, 36, 42), MOND in 3 classes (9, 36, 42). At $350/class USPTO filing fee = approximately $2,450 in government fees alone.

### 3.2 Filing basis

**Section 1(b) Intent-to-Use** for both marks. We are not yet using either mark in commerce. Section 1(b) locks our priority date today; we file Statements of Use after launch (within 36 months of Notice of Allowance, with extensions available).

### 3.3 Jurisdictions

1. **US (USPTO)** — file immediately, before incorporation if possible (founder can file individually and assign to Axion Inc. once formed).
1. **Madrid Protocol extension** — within 6 months of US filing date to preserve US priority date for international filings. Recommended target countries: EU (EUIPO), UK (UKIPO), Switzerland, Singapore, UAE, Japan.
1. **Defensive registrations in select jurisdictions** — consider direct national filings in any colony pilot host countries once those are identified.

### 3.4 Domain and corporate name parallels

Separate from trademark registration, please confirm:

- Delaware C-Corp name reservation: “Axion Inc.” or alternative if taken.
- Domain ownership: axion.fi, theaxion.com, axion.app.finance subdomain (already owned). Acquire mond.fi, themond.com if economical.
- Social handles: lock @axion and @mondcoin (or variants) on X, Telegram, Discord, GitHub.

### 4.7 On-chain reality check (critical for MOND analysis)

A deeper review of the actual blockchain state, conducted after the initial research, materially changed the risk profile for MOND. Trademark counsel should be aware:

**No live MOND token with meaningful activity has been deployed on any EVM chain.**

Specifically:

- **CryptoMondays MOND**: Per CryptoMondays’ own materials, the token is described as “a proposed digital asset” — future tense. The organization has not committed to MOND vs CMT as the final ticker, has not deployed a contract, has no holders, no trading volume, no liquidity. They are a real community (150K members), but no MOND token exists at protocol layer.
- **ERC-721 “mond” contract** at `0x4dc66a37d5a955b2ce6552509a25eed31bc1d9b8`: 0 total supply, 0 transactions, 0 holders. Dead contract, abandoned deployment.
- **Other MOND tokens**: searches surface only the above. No deployed MOND ERC-20 with material market cap, holders, or volume on Ethereum mainnet, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, or other major EVM chains as of date of this brief.

**Implication:** Axion would be the first meaningful MOND deployment on Base or any EVM chain. In token markets, first-mover often wins the namespace association in practice — by the time a later entrant arrives, the namespace is associated with the established project.

This shifts the strategic posture from “we are deploying into a crowded namespace” to “we are establishing the namespace.” It also means the urgency of filing first to lock priority date is real.

Please advise on the following before filing:

1. **AXION, LLC (Serial #88091906):** Who is this entity, what do they actually sell, are they currently using the mark in commerce, and what is the realistic risk they oppose our Class 9 or Class 42 filings? This is the single most material conflict to clear.
1. **Monad common-law claim:** Given Monad’s mainnet launch in Nov 2025 and active retail presence via Coinbase, do they have a colorable likelihood-of-confusion claim against MOND despite (a) MOND’s prior independent meaning (Modified Newtonian Dynamics, 1983), (b) MON ≠ MOND as a literal string, and (c) Monad operating on its own L1 while MOND would be on Base? What is your recommended posture — proactive coexistence agreement, ignore-and-defend, or alternative name?
1. **Mondu likelihood-of-confusion:** Mondu (Berlin) operates in B2B fintech with a phonetically adjacent mark. Same Class 36 space. What is the likelihood they oppose? Their mark is “Mondu” not “MOND” — does the suffix differentiation matter under the DuPont factors?
1. **CryptoMondays organizational claim:** Given the CryptoMondays community has 150K+ members globally but no deployed MOND token (token is described in their own materials as “proposed”), do they have any actionable common-law trademark rights to MOND in Class 36 or Class 9? Does informal future-tense use in marketing copy create priority?
1. **AXION, LLC vs Axion International (defunct):** Are AXION, LLC’s 2020 registrations in any way connected to or successor to Axion International Holdings (which liquidated 2016)? Would acquiring the liquidated entity’s residual IP be cheaper than litigating around AXION, LLC?

## 6. Litigation probability assessment (founder’s working estimate, for counsel’s calibration)

This is the founder’s rough estimate based on the research above. Counsel may revise.

|Event                                                          |Probability over 24 months|
|---------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
|Receive at least one C&D letter from any source                |30–40%                    |
|C&D dies after a one-page response from counsel (no escalation)|80%+ of C&Ds received     |
|USPTO opposition filed against our application                 |15–25%                    |
|Litigation actually filed against Axion Inc.                   |~5%                       |
|Litigation lost on the merits                                  |<2%                       |

**Why these numbers are this low (founder’s reasoning):**

- US trademark protection is class-specific and confusion-based, not name-based. Apple Corps v. Apple Computer coexisted for 30 years before Apple Music brought them into the same class. Delta Airlines, Delta Faucets, Delta Dental coexist because nobody confuses an airline with a tap. Identical names in different markets is the norm, not the exception.
- The substantive defenses are strong: MOND has independent prior meaning from 1983; AXION’s most-concerning conflict (AXION LLC 2020) appears to be a small entity unlikely to have litigation budget.
- The most-resourced potential adversary (Monad) faces a defensible case on the merits and would suffer reputational cost from being seen to attack a smaller crypto project on thin grounds.
- Mondee’s MOND is a stock ticker, not a trademark — stock tickers are not in themselves protectable marks.

**Reserve budget recommendation:** ~$10K–30K for one opposition proceeding if it happens. Bet-the-company litigation is highly unlikely.

## 9. Strategic context (for counsel’s situational awareness)

Axion Inc. is building a blockchain-based parallel economic protocol issuing MOND as a closed-loop unit of account, with citizens receiving share dividends rather than wages. The macro thesis (“The Great Collision”) positions Axion as crisis infrastructure for AI-driven displacement and sovereign debt monetization. Fundraising is via Reg CF on Wefunder targeting ~$1M at $10M valuation cap. The brand’s intended register is institutional/heritage rather than retail-crypto — comparable in tone to SpaceX or a modern central bank, not a memecoin.

Three implications for trademark strategy:

1. **Reputational sensitivity is high.** A loud trademark fight that surfaces “Axion sued by Monad” would be damaging during fundraise. Quiet, well-prepared filings matter.
1. **International posture matters early.** Colony deployment is likely in multiple jurisdictions within 24–36 months. Madrid Protocol coverage isn’t optional.
1. **Closed-loop design reduces external trademark friction.** MOND is non-tradeable outside the colony economy, enforced at the smart contract level. It is not listed on CEXs or available to retail outside the protocol. This narrows the consumer-confusion surface meaningfully — the typical channel through which MOND would meet a Monad holder doesn’t exist.
