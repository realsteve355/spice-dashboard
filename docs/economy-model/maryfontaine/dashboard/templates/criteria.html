Success criteria

What does "the colony delivers steady ground" mean numerically? This page pins it down. Success is not a single threshold — it's a tiered comparison against (a) doing nothing in the colony, and (b) just holding USD outside it. The real-world question is: under The Transition scenario, does the colony deliver more stable purchasing power than a citizen would experience in the wider USD economy?

1. The headline metric

Real purchasing power: how many baskets of goods one citizen's monthly UBI buys.

At founding (parity, $1/S, basket = 28 S), 100 S of UBI buys 3.57 baskets. That's the reference point for "what citizens can afford with no work, no dividends, no external income — just the colony's UBI."

Outside the colony — someone holding US dollars — the reference is the basket cost in USD. At founding, $100 USD also buys 3.57 baskets ($28 each). Under The Transition, by Y10 the basket costs $58 in USD; $100 USD buys 1.72 baskets — a 52% loss in real terms. That 1.72 figure is the "do nothing, stay outside" floor. Anything above it = colony beats USD; anything below it = colony underperforms USD.

2. Success tiers

Tested by Y10 real purchasing power (baskets per 100 S UBI) under The Transition scenario at default 10% scale:

Full steady ground
Y10 PP ≥ 3.40 baskets · basket-cost-in-S ≤ 29.4 · < 5% PP loss
The peg holds throughout. Citizens experience effectively flat real purchasing power — the colony has fully insulated them from external chaos. This is the goal but a high bar. Likely requires a stack of mitigations: LAT, refinanced mortgages, possibly capped cashout.
Strong defence
Y10 PP ≥ 3.00 · basket-cost-in-S ≤ 33.3 · < 16% PP loss · ≥ 1.7× external
Peg holds for most of the decade with mild compression in late years. Citizens lose noticeable but bearable purchasing power — but still 75% better off than holding USD outside. This is a credible "colony delivered" outcome.
Partial defence
Y10 PP ≥ 2.40 · basket-cost-in-S ≤ 41.7 · < 33% PP loss · ≥ 1.4× external
Peg breaks mid-decade; basket cost in S inflates moderately. Citizens lose ~30% real purchasing power but still meaningfully better than USD-only (1.72 baskets). Defensible as "colony helped" but not the design target.
Weak defence
Y10 PP ≥ 1.72 · basket-cost-in-S ≤ 58 · < 52% PP loss · ≥ external
Citizens fare roughly the same inside the colony as outside it — colony provides no meaningful relief. Worth knowing as a pass/fail line: anything below this is worse than doing nothing.
Worse than doing nothing
Y10 PP < 1.72 · basket-cost-in-S > 58 · > 52% PP loss
Colony citizens are worse off than people outside the colony holding USD. The design has failed: the SPICE economy compounds the problem rather than relieving it. Configurations that produce this need fundamental rethinking, not parameter tuning.

3. Secondary signals

Beyond the headline PP metric, three additional dimensions matter:

Distribution of welfare

Median PP ≥ 2.40 means little if the bottom decile is at 0.5 (subsistence-level destitution). The dashboard's deciles chart shows whether the gain is broadly shared. Threshold: p10 should not fall below 0.7 baskets/UBI — the floor below which a citizen on UBI alone genuinely can't make ends meet.

Reserve adequacy + stability

The Fisc reserve should not deplete catastrophically. Threshold: reserve ≥ $5M (founding level) at Y10. Below that the colony has no shock-absorption left for unexpected events. Better: reserve grows over the decade, indicating sustainable boundary balance.

Strivers economy / dynamism

Citizens transitioning ubi_only → striver indicates the colony is producing economic mobility. Threshold: at least 5% of ubi_only_choice citizens transition to striver over 10 years. Below that, the colony is a stagnant safety-net rather than a vibrant economy. (Note: company founding by strivers is deferred to Phase 3, so the striver count alone undercounts the dynamism.)

Boundary balance

Long-run boundary flows (USDC inflows from exports + remote workers, vs outflows for imports + mortgages + cashouts) should be sustainable. Threshold: cumulative net boundary flow ≥ 0 over Y4-Y10 — the colony shouldn't be slowly draining its reserve to keep the lights on.

4. Anti-criteria — what we DON'T optimise for

Some metrics are tempting but shouldn't drive design choices:

  • Maximum reserve growth. Hoarding USDC isn't the goal. A colony with $30M reserve but PP loss 40% has failed; a colony with $5M reserve and PP loss 5% has succeeded. The reserve is a means.
  • Minimum S supply. Aggressive S burning (high LAT, high S-tax) suppresses S supply growth, which helps the cover ratio — but at the cost of taking S away from companies + citizens. Need balance.
  • Maximum dividend distribution. Workers being wealthy is good, but if it triggers excessive cashout (capital flight), it undermines reserve defence. The design should be politically attractive without being self-undermining.
  • Peg held forever at 28. A small late-decade compression to 30 or 31 is acceptable if the alternative is severe rationing or ultra-high LAT. Perfect peg defence isn't worth ruinous side-effects.

5. Open questions

The criteria above are working assumptions, not received wisdom. Open for debate:

  • Cover-ratio target of 0.30. Asserted, not derived. Try 0.20 vs 0.40 in the sweep and see which is more compatible with steady ground.
  • 52% external PP loss baseline. Comes from The Transition basket trajectory. If our assumption of how steep the AI deflation gets is wrong, the floor moves. Sensitivity-test by trying milder Transitions.
  • 10-year horizon. The credibility test is a 10-year decade, but real colonies will run for longer. A configuration that works at 10y but fails at 20y isn't really steady ground. Future task: extend horizon to 240 months.
  • Founding mortgage burden. Joe-the-Honda-worker can't afford his $1,400/month mortgage on UBI + dividend alone, even with the corrected (Honda-Inc-doesn't-extract) model. Mortgage refi is a workaround; the real question is whether founding mortgage sizes are realistic for SPICE-income households. See /families for Joe's trajectory.
  • The strivers economy. Phase 3 deferred — striver citizens currently can't found new companies in the sim. So the "dynamism" measure is partially synthetic. A real colony's PP outcome would be lifted by new exporters founded over time.

Use these criteria as the rubric while playing with the dashboard. A configuration isn't "the answer" until it clears strong defence at minimum, and ideally hits full steady ground. We're not there yet.
