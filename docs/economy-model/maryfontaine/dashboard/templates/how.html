How the MaryFontaine simulator works

A walk-through of what happens when you click Run simulation. Two phases — founding setup (one shot) and the monthly tick (120 times). About 15 distinct mechanisms move S and USDC around the colony each month, recording every transaction. The Fisc tries to keep the basket of goods costing 28 S; under stress, it has to compress the rate; that compression is the headline finding.

1. Overview

When you click Run simulation on the dashboard, the simulator does two things: builds the colony from scratch using your variant number, then runs N monthly ticks (12 to 120 depending on the slider) to simulate the colony's economic life over time. Every transaction is recorded; monthly state snapshots are written; the dashboard charts read from this output.

The simulator is not "agent-based" in the strict ABM sense — it's transaction-level with per-agent state and rule-based decisions. Every citizen has their own wallet, behavioural type, household, and state; every company has its own balance, equity holdings, and revenue capacity. But citizens don't make AI-driven decisions — they follow rules whose parameters you can tune.

The point of the simulator is not to grade a fixed design. It's to search for the configuration of mechanisms that delivers steady ground during the scarcity-to-abundance transition. The sliders on the dashboard are the search levers.

2. Phase A — Founding setup

Founding takes about half a second. The DB is wiped and rebuilt from your variant number. Six steps:

2.1 Generate citizens

The simulator creates 3,900 individuals at 10% scale (10% of the full MaryFontaine target of 39,000). Each citizen gets:

  • An archetype drawn from 12 categories — children under 18 (23.1%), Honda assembly workers (8.2%), Honda admin (2.1%), other manufacturing (6.4%), healthcare workers (4.6%), education workers (3.1%), retail/services (10.3%), small business owners (3.8%), sole traders (5.1%), remote workers (3.8%), retirees (21.8%), ubi-only-by-choice (7.7%). Proportions match the spec to within ±0.5%.
  • A behavioural type — saver / balanced / striver / spender — drawn from a per-archetype distribution. Honda assembly workers are 30% saver / 50% balanced / 15% striver / 5% spender; strivers are over-represented among small business owners; retirees are 60% saver. This affects how they spend and save later.
  • A starting age — children 0–17, retirees 65–90, everyone else 18–65.
  • For remote workers: a monthly external USD income drawn from {60% × $50K/yr, 30% × $75K/yr, 10% × $120K/yr}.
  • For retirees: a 30% chance of an external pension of $500–$2,000/month USD; the other 70% rely on permanent equity dividends + savings.

2.2 Form households

Citizens get grouped into ~2,200 households. Children must live with adults. The mix is roughly 1/3 family-with-kids, 1/4 single-adult, 1/6 couple-no-kids, 5% single-parent. Each household gets:

  • A housing type: 30% own outright, 35% own with a mortgage, 25% rent inside the colony, 10% rent from an external landlord.
  • For the 35% with mortgages: a remaining balance ($50K–$300K), interest rate (4–7%), and remaining months (60–360). The monthly payment is computed via the standard level-payment mortgage formula. This becomes a USD outflow every month — a major reserve drain.
  • A basket consumption multiplier — 1.0 per adult + 0.5 per child. So a family of 4 (2 adults + 2 kids) consumes 3.0× a single adult.
  • A discretionary propensity based on the primary citizen's behavioural type — savers spend 20% of surplus on non-basket items; spenders 80%.

2.3 Generate companies

~85 catalogue companies (Honda, MCC, hospital, supermarket, etc.) plus 199 sole-trader companies (one per sole-trader citizen). Each company gets:

  • A sector ("automotive_manufacturing", "healthcare", "retail_grocery", etc.)
  • One or more basket categories served ("energy", "food", "goods", "services") — used by the supplier picker later
  • A max revenue per month in S — sets the capacity ceiling for how much business it can do
  • An export and import baseline in USD per month — Honda exports $375K/month, the hospital imports $15K/month for medical supplies, etc.
  • A CFO policy — conservative / aggressive_dividend / growth_focused — affects how much working capital it keeps before paying dividends.

2.4 Allocate equity

This is the careful bit. Honda gets the canonical 60% Honda Inc / 5% colony stakeholders / 35% workers split (assembly workers 0.5 shares each, admin 1.5 each). Sole traders get 100% permanent equity to themselves. Other companies get 1–3 small business owners holding ~80% permanent + workers from the relevant archetype holding ~20% time-limited. Total: 1,148 equity holdings.

2.5 Wallets

One per citizen, one per company, one for the Fisc, one for "external" (the rest of the world). Companies start with working capital (~40% of their max-revenue-per-month, representing pre-colony assets converted to S at parity — companies inherit this from the pre-colony environment). The Fisc starts with $5M USDC reserve. Total founding S supply is ~$2.5M held by companies.

2.6 Household preferences

This is the only part of founding that happens at simulation-load rather than at DB-build: each household draws a Dirichlet-style preference vector over the companies in each basket category. So household #47 might have a 35% preference for Diner 3, 25% for Cafe 1, etc. These determine which companies that household picks for each category over the 10 years.

3. Phase B — The monthly tick

3.1 Order of operations

For each month from 1 to 120, the simulator executes 15 steps in this order. Order matters — for example, citizens have to spend before companies can compute distributable surplus, because spending is what generates company revenue this month.

3.2 The 15 steps

Step 1
Read the external environment
From the scenario table (e.g. Transition), look up this month's USD prices for energy, food, goods, services. Computed from annual % rates by geometric compounding: monthly_factor = (1 + annual_rate)^(1/12). Sum gives total basket USD this month. Under Transition, energy starts at $8 and ends at $22.74; goods start at $5 and end at $0.36 (-93%).
Step 2
Compute the Fisc rate
The Fisc tries to maintain basket cost in S = 28. So target_rate = basket_USD / 28. But it also has to defend the cover ratio: USDC reserve must cover at least 30% of (S supply × rate). If the reserve isn't enough, the rate is forced down (compressed) — basket cost in S then exceeds 28, and that's flagged as the stress signal.
This is where the Transition peg-break shows up. At Y3 the reserve can no longer back the target rate, so the Fisc has to devalue S.
Step 3
UBI mint
Every living citizen gets 100 S minted from the Fisc (configurable per slider). At 3,900 citizens that's 390K S/month, $4.7M/year. The USDC reserve doesn't move — S is minted out of nothing. Each mint is recorded as a ubi_mint transaction. Honda assembly worker Joe gets his 100 S right here.
Step 4
External income
Remote workers receive their USD wages from outside the colony. The Fisc accepts the USD into the reserve and mints the equivalent S to the worker at the current rate. A remote worker on $66K/yr would get $5,500 USD → 5,500/$1.05 ≈ 5,238 S in their wallet, and the reserve grows by $5,500. Same mechanic for retirees with pensions.
Step 5
Exporter revenue
Honda's exports come in: $375K of USD goes into the Fisc reserve, Honda's wallet gets credited 375K/$1.05 ≈ 357K S. Same for the four Bellefontaine manufacturers. Total monthly USD inflow from exports at 10% scale is ~$500K. Honda-shock scenarios scale this down at month 39+.
Step 6
MCC bills citizens
The MCC (utilities only — water, electricity, sewer, internet) charges each household based on adult count. Single adult = 30 S; each additional adult = +15 S. A 2-adult household = 45 S. Charge gets debited from the primary citizen's wallet, credited to the MCC's wallet. If they don't have enough S, they pay what they can — a stress signal.
Step 7
MCC bills companies
Same mechanic but for companies. Honda pays 80 + 0.5% × $600K-capacity ≈ 3,080 S to the MCC.
Step 7.5
MPC (only if on)
Each non-MCC company pays a configurable % of its max-revenue-per-month to the Fisc. The S is destroyed — taken out of supply. This reduces the cover-ratio denominator, strengthening the Fisc's defence of the peg.
With MPC 5%, Honda pays 30K S/month into the burn pile. This is the lever that does most of the work in the headline mitigation case.
Step 8
Citizen consumption (the supplier picker)
Each household goes shopping. Total spend = 28 S × household multiplier × current basket-cost-in-S adjustment. For a 2-adult household at parity, that's 56 S/month. Split across the 4 categories by their weights (energy 28.6%, food 32.1%, goods 17.9%, services 21.4%).

For each category, the supplier picker runs: it weights every active in-category company by household preference × spare capacity, then picks one weighted-randomly. The S transfers from the citizen's wallet to the supplier's wallet, the supplier records revenue, the supermarket's spare capacity drops.

If S-tax is on, a fraction of every purchase is taxed and burned at the Fisc. If a category has no available capacity (every in-category company is saturated), the spend is recorded as unmet demand — a signal that supply isn't keeping up.

Step 9
Citizen housing
Three subcases:
  • Mortgage: Monthly payment of, say, $1,400 USD is converted to S at the current rate (1,400/$1.05 ≈ 1,333 S). S balance drops; S is burned at the Fisc; Fisc's USDC reserve drops by $1,400 (sent to the external bank). At 746 mortgage households averaging $1,500/month, this is ~$13M/year of USDC outflow at 10% scale. This is the biggest reason the reserve doesn't grow.
  • External rent: Same mechanism — S burned, USDC out.
  • Internal rent: Different — picks an internal services-category supplier (a colony landlord), pays in S, no USDC flow.
If mortgage-refinance-to-S is on, the S still burns but the USDC stays put — the colony's "bank" effectively bought the mortgage from the external bank. One of the strongest mitigations.
Step 10
Companies pay imports
Each importing company (Honda, supermarket, hospital) needs USD to buy goods/services from outside. They pay in S; the Fisc burns the S and ships USDC out. Honda imports $208K/month — $208K leaves the reserve every month. If a company doesn't have enough S, it defaults — recorded as an import_default_count for that company that month.
Step 11
Operating costs
Currently a no-op. The spec implies wages would be paid here, but in the SPICE design wages are replaced by dividends + UBI, so there's no separate wage flow. Operating cost is implicitly captured by the working-capital target in step 12.
Step 12
Dividend distribution
The company-to-citizen wealth flow.
  • For each company: compute working capital target (e.g. conservative CFO = 1.5× monthly revenue capacity × 55% op-cost factor). Surplus above this is distributable.
  • Distribution goes only to citizen + colony-stakeholder shares. External equity holders (Honda Inc, who owns 60% of the Honda factory for governance / sale purposes) do NOT receive monthly dividend cashouts. Their value flow is the cars they buy as an export customer (step 5), not a dividend extracted from the colony.
  • So at the Honda factory: distributable surplus is split between workers (35% time-limited shares) and colony stakeholders (5% permanent shares). Workers receive 35/40 = 87.5% of surplus; colony stakeholders 12.5%. Honda Inc's 60% claim sits on the books for ownership / sale purposes but doesn't materialise as monthly cash.
  • Citizens see S land in their wallet at month-end. Workers at exporting companies (Honda, Bellefontaine Mfg) accumulate substantial S quickly — they're the colony's wealthiest citizens.
Step 13
Citizen behavioural decisions
Each citizen with surplus S above subsistence×1.5 (= 75 S) increments their surplus_streak counter. They cash out a fraction of the surplus to USDC based on their behavioural type — savers cash out 10%, balanced 5%, strivers 2%, spenders 0%. The cashout multiplier slider scales all of this. Cashout = S burned + USDC out of reserve.

Strivers with balance > 5× threshold also flip a has_made_first_equity_purchase flag — the precursor to an archetype transition.

Step 14
Archetype transitions
Check each citizen:
  • ubi_only_choice → striver: needs 6 consecutive months of surplus + has-made-first-equity-purchase + balance > 3× threshold
  • worker → striver: same but stricter (12 months, 5× threshold)
  • striver → small_business_owner: deferred to Phase 3 (depends on company founding)
  • Anyone with sustained job loss → ubi_only_choice: also Phase 3 (depends on company failure)
When a transition fires, record it in archetype_history.
Step 15
Snapshots
Write a row to fisc_state every month (rate, reserve, supply, basket cost, cover ratio, compressed flag). Write per-citizen and per-company snapshots only at year-end (months 12, 24, ...) — keeps the DB at a manageable ~50K rows instead of 500K.

5. What's not yet simulated (deferred to Phase 3+)

  • Births and deaths. Population is static within a run.
  • Company founding by strivers. The flag fires (has_made_first_equity_purchase) but no actual new companies appear.
  • Company failure. Companies don't go out of business even if persistently insolvent.
  • Equity transfers. No second-hand equity market.
  • Within-month timing. All settlement happens at month-end; intra-month liquidity stress is detected via min_s_balance_within_month but doesn't cause failures yet.
  • Price competition. Companies don't compete on price — supplier choice is preference × capacity.
  • Macro feedback. The colony does not affect external markets.

If you find that mitigations are insufficient under The Transition, these deferred mechanisms (especially the strivers economy producing new exporters) might shift the picture meaningfully. That's Phase 3.
