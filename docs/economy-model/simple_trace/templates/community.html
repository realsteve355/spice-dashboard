A community of 1,000 — the robot's wage

How the colony's output splits into wages, MAC and profit as automation advances

The idea

The value a community produces in a year — its output — is shared out three ways: as wages to people who work, as profit to the owners of capital, and — once automation arrives — as the MAC: the robot's wage. The MAC is a business expense that sits in the accounts where wages used to be; the firm pays it on the automation that replaced its workers, and it is handed to the displaced as UBI.

So the identity is simply wages + MAC + profit = output. As employment falls, the wage share drains away — but the MAC picks it up and the displaced still have income to spend, so demand never falls. And because a basic basket costs less than the wage it replaces, the firm keeps the difference: profit rises. UBI is funded by construction; the only question is whether profit stays positive — i.e. whether firms remain viable. Worked below for 1,000 people.
