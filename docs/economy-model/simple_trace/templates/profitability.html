MAC sufficiency — does the charge fund the UBI?

The core test. The MAC pool (green) sits above total UBI cost for all 180,000 adults (amber), with the gap — the headroom — widening over time.
MAC pool is the revenue raised by the Market Access Charge (k × profit pool). Total UBI cost is the cost of paying UBI to all 180,000 adults. In the earliest displacement years the MAC may not fully cover UBI on its own — a temporary sovereign top-up or deficit is expected during the transition. Coverage then improves steadily and becomes strongly sufficient by mid-period as profits grow.

Key metrics — MAC coverage of UBI

Figures in real (today's prices) terms, scaled for a county of 180,000 adults. UBI cost is universal (every adult). MAC pool = k × profit pool; MAC coverage = MAC pool ÷ UBI cost.
Note on early years: in Year 0–3 the headline coverage assumes the full attributable profit pool; in practice it ramps up as the charge and the attributed base come online, so some transitional sovereign support may be needed before the MAC stands alone.

Productivity growth trajectory

Productivity growth starts at ~2.8%/yr and accelerates to ~5%/yr after widespread humanoid-robot deployment in the mid-2030s, then moderates — averaging ~3.7–4% over the 20 years. This drives both the basket deflation and the expansion of the profit pool.

The argument

What the profit pool represents

The Corporate Profit Pool is the total real economic profit (broad EBITDA / operating surplus) attributable to companies whose goods and services are consumed by MaryFontaine residents — local firms plus the proportional share of national and global companies. At ~$14,400 per adult in Year 0 it is consistent with US averages.

MAC sufficiency

The MAC recycles a portion of profits into UBI, sustaining demand. Coverage starts adequate and grows strongly over time as automation expands the profit pool — from ~193% in Year 0 to ~341% by Year 20. In the earliest displacement years some transitional sovereign support may be needed; thereafter the MAC funds UBI on its own with increasing headroom.

The model assumes UBI is spent at high propensity, so recycled income returns to firms as revenue — the closed loop that keeps the profit pool on its sustainable path.
