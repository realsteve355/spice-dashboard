# Automation cost forecasts — what the bulls actually say

The aim of this doc is to present **what the people building the technology
forecast** about cost trajectories by category, on the timeline. Source-
attributed. Skeptic counterpoints included where they exist (Acemoglu).

This is the data Steve asked for: **forecast first, then we figure out how
the colony interacts with it**.

---

## The convergent thesis (Musk, Altman, Saylor, Diamandis, Seba, Kurzweil)

> Within 10–20 years, the marginal cost of producing most goods and services
> approaches zero. Energy and intelligence become utilities priced like water.
> Land and a few inherently scarce resources are the exception — they may rise.

These six don't agree on every detail but converge on the headline. The
counterpoint is Daron Acemoglu (MIT/NBER, 2024), who estimates only 0.5–0.7%
TFP gain over 10 years from AI. Worth surfacing as the dissenting view.

---

## By forecaster, what they predict

### Elon Musk (Tesla, xAI)
- **10–20 years until work is optional, money irrelevant.** "Universal high income — you can have whatever you want."
- AI smarter than all humans combined by 2030.
- More humanoid robots than humans on Earth.
- **Tesla Optimus: $20–30K target price** vs $30–50K/yr human labor. Labor cost replacement <1 year payback. 10M/year production capacity planned at Giga Texas.
- "Marginal cost of production approaches zero. Goods and services become extremely abundant. Poverty eradicated."

### Sam Altman (OpenAI)
- **"Cost of intelligence and energy on path to near-zero. Won't get all the way there this decade, but by 2030 it will become clear."**
- "Anyone in 2035 should be able to marshall the intellectual capacity equivalent to everyone in 2025. Everyone should have access to unlimited genius."
- Intelligence sold as utility — "like water or electricity, flooding the global market."
- **"The price of luxury goods and a few inherently limited resources like land may rise even more dramatically."** (This is exactly Steve's land thesis from a different mouth.)
- AI killing the labor-capital balance — admits "nobody knows what to do about it."

### Michael Saylor (Strategy)
- **100x productivity rise from AI + automation.**
- "Era of excess rather than shortage."
- 10-year window for investors to position.
- "AI creates wealth, Bitcoin protects it" — emphasises asset ownership over income.
- Dollar at 7%/yr debasement vs hard assets.

### Peter Diamandis (Abundance, Solve Everything)
- "Solve Everything by 2035" — explicit decade timeline.
- **Phase 3 (2032–2035): energy, climate, food, and infrastructure become "dial-tone-reliable utilities."**
- 2030 vision: "physical world has liquefied" — subscription to "Normal Liver Function" instead of buying products.
- Vertical farming today: 80% less land, 90% less water, 100% fewer pesticides.

### Ray Kurzweil
- **Singularity 2045.** Reaffirmed in 2024.
- Early 2030s: non-biological computation exceeds all biological human intelligence combined.
- Computers pass Turing test by 2029.
- 2030s: brain-cloud connection.
- "Reversible energy enables the suite of technologies; near-zero energy costs are a precondition."

### RethinkX / Tony Seba
- **Five foundational sectors (info, energy, food, transport, materials) disrupted 2020–2033 with costs falling 10x or more.**
- **Energy:** solar+wind+battery cheaper than fossil by 2030 in virtually all populated regions.
- **Transport:** 95% of US miles in autonomous EV fleets by 2030. $1T/yr returned to consumers.
- **Food:** proteins 5x cheaper by 2030. US cattle herd halved. Cattle industry bankrupt by 2030.
- 90% emissions cut by 2035, net zero by 2040.
- Framework: change is non-linear. Long equilibrium then sudden phase change.

### Daron Acemoglu (MIT, NBER w32487, 2024) — DISSENTING
- **0.53–0.66% total TFP gain over 10 years from AI.** Up to 1% of US economic output over the next decade.
- Only 20% of US labour tasks exposed; even fewer profitably automatable.
- Methodologically grounded in Hulten's theorem.
- Argues AI hype is overdone; productivity gains modest, inequality rising.

## What this means for the SPICE narrative

The Bull thesis (held by people who are *building* the technology) is consistent
with the SPICE founding observation: by ~2045, almost everything except land
and a few luxury goods is free.

The question for SPICE is not *whether* it gets there — six independent
voices say it does — but *how the colony bridges the transition*.

The transition lasts 15–25 years. During it:
- Some categories collapse fast (energy, intelligence, info goods)
- Some collapse moderately (food, manufactured goods, transport)
- Some resist (services, healthcare initially)
- One INFLATES (land, deferred to a separate model)

The SPICE colony's job during the transition is to:
1. Levy automation at a level that funds welfare for displaced citizens
2. Build the company-equity infrastructure that lets citizens accumulate wealth (path to land ownership)
3. Reach welfare-capable (M1) and full UBI-capable (M2) milestones on a defensible timeline
