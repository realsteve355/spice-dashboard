Tax treatment of the MAC and UBI

How the Market Access Charge and the basic income sit within existing US tax frameworks · a Fisc feature

The Axion system is designed to integrate with existing US tax frameworks while reducing long-term pressure on federal welfare spending.

Market Access Charge (MAC)

  • The customer pays the normal product or service price plus applicable state and local sales tax. The MAC is not added to the customer's receipt.
  • The MAC is calculated on the base transaction value and automatically skimmed from the seller's proceeds through the Mond / Fisc system.
  • From a tax perspective, the MAC forms part of the total sale value for sales tax purposes.
Example

A customer buys an iPhone for $1,000 + applicable sales tax (e.g. $80), paying a total of $1,080. The MAC ($12 in this example) is calculated on the $1,000 base price and is automatically skimmed from Apple's proceeds by the Fisc. Apple records the $12 MAC as a deductible business expense. The Fisc separately remits sales tax on the $12 MAC portion to the state.

Basic Income (UBI) distributed by the Fisc

  • UBI is considered taxable income to recipients at the federal and state level.
  • The Fisc, acting as the payer of benefits, issues annual 1099 forms to recipients and reports totals to the IRS and state tax authorities.
  • The Fisc will facilitate voluntary tax withholding from UBI payments unless citizens opt out.
  • Recipients are responsible for reporting UBI on their individual tax returns and paying any applicable income tax.
  • UBI payments are generally not subject to FICA (Social Security and Medicare) taxes, as they are not earned wages.

Overall impact

  • The system does not create a new layer of taxation on consumers but redirects value from automation gains into basic income.
  • By locally funding basic living standards through the MAC, the model has the potential to reduce demand for federal and state welfare programs over time.
  • Social Security and Medicare eligibility and benefits remain unchanged and continue to be based on earned income and existing federal rules.

This structure maintains compliance with current tax law while enabling a smoother transition to an automation-abundant economy.
