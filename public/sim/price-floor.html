How far do prices actually fall?

Solving for the price floor once a UBI has to be funded out of those same prices

The question

The usual story is that AI and robots drive prices down to a small fraction of today's — manufactured goods to ~10%, and so on. That assumes the whole productivity saving is passed through to lower prices. But there is a catch: the workers replaced by machines need an income to buy those goods, or there is no one to sell to. That income — a basic income — is funded by a charge on production (the Market Access Charge), and that charge is part of what everyone buys.

So the price can't fall below the level needed to fund the very demand that buys it. Prices and the basic income are drawn from the same productivity gain — you can take it as cheaper prices, or as income to keep people buying, but not fully as both. This page solves for where the price actually settles:

price = (cost after productivity) ÷ (1 − share of spending that is basic income)
the top falls as machines get cheaper · the bottom pushes the price back up as more people live on the basic income
