The MAC loop — how a post-automation economy closes

When robots make everything for free, how does a basic income fund itself? By being skimmed every time the money changes hands.

The problem, and the trick

Once robots make everything at zero cost, no one earns wages — so the only way people can buy anything is a basic income. But if a single firm hands out the money and then sells to those same people, it just gets its own money back and is left carrying its costs:

Π = M − (COGS + M) = −COGS
hand out M, collect M back, and your profit is just minus your costs — the loop stalls

The fix isn't a higher price — it's velocity. The treasury (the Fisc) takes a cut — the Market Access Charge — every time money is spent at an automated firm. One pass of spending only claws back the rate (say 80%). But the money is spent again — businesses buying from other businesses — and gets skimmed a second time. Add the passes up and the Fisc recovers the whole basic income, ready to pay out again. The numbers below close at exactly 1:1.

What you can change

Pass 1 — the basic income is spent

The Fisc injects the basic income; citizens spend it. The MAC skims each automated sale (the human sector pays nothing). This pass alone leaves the Fisc short — the gap is what the firms keep.

Pass 2 — the money is spent again (business-to-business)

The human firms buy automated compute and logistics to do their work. Those sales get skimmed too — recapturing the gap left by the first pass and closing the loop.
