The argument

Why profits surge initially

Automation dramatically reduces unit costs — labour, energy, errors, overhead. In many sectors prices fall more slowly than costs, held up by market power, branding, network effects and intellectual property. The result is significant margin expansion, concentrated in the 2026–2035 window.

The demand problem

If profits accrue overwhelmingly to capital owners, consumer purchasing power eventually lags behind production capacity. This is the classic Henry Ford insight: workers and consumers need sufficient income to buy the output. Without intervention, revenue growth slows and profit growth eventually stalls — the unchecked line above.

The Market Access Charge solution

The MAC recycles a portion of automation-driven profits back into consumer hands via UBI. This maintains healthy demand — and therefore revenue — while still allowing strong, sustainable profit growth. It creates a stable, self-reinforcing abundance loop.

MaryFontaine design insight

The colony is deliberately designed to handle the upper end of the Bull unemployment scenario (~75% optional work by Year 20) while keeping corporate profitability healthy through the MAC mechanism.
