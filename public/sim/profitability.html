Corporate Profitability in the Age of AI Abundance

A targeted Market Access Charge on profit-per-employee, funding a phased transition to full UBI

MaryFontaine — county context

  • Population ~300,000 (~180,000 adults)
  • The Corporate Profit Pool represents total real economic profit attributable to companies serving MaryFontaine residents — local firms plus the proportional share of national and global companies (Amazon, Walmart, Google, Apple, and so on).

The central mechanism — a targeted Market Access Charge

Each company pays a rate that rises with its profit-per-employee. Heavily automated firms (high profit, few employees) pay a large share of profit; labour-intensive and human-centric businesses pay very little. This protects human work while funding UBI.

The argument

What the profit pool represents

The Corporate Profit Pool is the total real economic profit (broad EBITDA / operating surplus) attributable to companies whose goods and services are consumed by MaryFontaine residents — local firms plus the proportional share of national and global companies. At ~$14,400 per adult in Year 0 it is consistent with US averages.

A targeted charge that protects human work

Because the rate scales with profit-per-employee, the charge falls hardest on hyper-automated firms and barely touches labour-intensive, human-centric businesses. This protects new post-AI industries and the jobs that remain, while still raising the revenue that funds UBI.

A phased transition to full UBI

Means-tested support carries the early displacement years; full universal UBI begins at the Year-8 inflection (~28% unemployment). From there the MAC pool covers UBI with increasing headroom — from ~205% at the inflection to ~341% by Year 20 — as automation expands the profit pool. A low-tax, automated abundance where the profit-per-employee formula keeps human work protected and UBI sustainable.
