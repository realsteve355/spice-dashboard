The argument

What the profit pool represents

The Corporate Profit Pool is the total real economic profit (broad EBITDA / operating surplus) attributable to companies whose goods and services are consumed by MaryFontaine residents — local firms plus the proportional share of national and global companies (Amazon, Google, Apple, Walmart, and so on). At ~$14,400 per adult in Year 0 it is consistent with US averages.

Why profits grow strongly

Automation reduces unit costs faster than the research Aggregate Basket prices fall. The gap is margin, and it expands — concentrated in the first ten to fifteen years.

The demand challenge & MAC role

Unchecked profit concentration risks weakening consumer demand: as employment falls, purchasing power follows, and revenue — then profit — stalls. The Market Access Charge recycles a portion of profits back into UBI, sustaining demand and revenue while still allowing healthy corporate profitability.

MAC sufficiency proof

At k = 0.22 the MAC pool fully funds the projected UBI at every stage of the 20-year ramp, with coverage rising from ~193% in Year 0 to ~341% by Year 20 — increasing headroom as automation advances. Use the slider to confirm sufficiency holds across the k = 0.15–0.30 range.

MaryFontaine insight

The colony is deliberately designed to handle high automation (~75% optional work by Year 20) while keeping corporate profits strong and UBI sustainable through the MAC mechanism.

The model assumes UBI is spent at high propensity, so recycled income returns to firms as revenue — the closed loop that keeps the profit pool on its sustainable path.
